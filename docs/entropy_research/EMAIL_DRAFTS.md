## 1. Kelley School finance professor

**Subject:** Does a failed firm's market share predict how its rivals react? A short pre-registered test

Dear Professor [Name],

I am a junior in Finance and Informatics at IU. I have written a short working paper on
what happens to a firm's rivals when it collapses. The finding I trust most is a
negative one. In 266 isolated crashes of S&P 500 firms, the failed firm's market share
sizes the rivals' *first-day* fall, but it has no out-of-sample power to predict their
reaction: the cross-validated R² is below zero in every sample. It also did not predict
anything in 40 new US and Indian collapses that I labelled from the news *before* looking
at returns (a pre-registered test, committed publicly in advance).

My share measure is each firm's share of trading value, because I have no revenue data.
**Would you treat replacing it with Compustat revenue shares as the single most important
fix, or would you prioritise something else first?**

I have a two-page summary and the full draft if useful.

## 2. Tsinghua professor visiting / at IU

**Subject:** A pre-registered test of contagion after firm failures, and an emerging-market question

Dear Professor [Name],

I am an IU undergraduate working on a paper about whether a firm's collapse spreads to its
rivals. I wrote a rule in advance: rivals should lose when they share a critical
dependency with the failed firm, such as the same funding market, depositors or owner. I
then tested it on 40 new US and Indian collapses. The surprise was how *rare* such cases
are. Labelled strictly from day-0 news, only 4 of 40 qualified, which was too few to test
the rule. Rivals of the two US bank rescues in 2008–09 fell hard, but so did rivals of
other banks in the same weeks, and the two Indian cases barely moved. In India, firms in the failed firm's business group fell in all three cases.

**Is there a Chinese setting you would suggest where shared-funding failures are frequent
enough to run the same pre-registered test properly? I was wondering about the 2020–21
state-owned-enterprise bond defaults or the trust-company failures.**

Thank you for any pointer.

Best regards,
Mrigay Pathak

## 3. Uncle at NTU (sustainability)

**Subject:** Your field's idea of resilience vs efficiency, applied to firm failures

Dear [Uncle's name],

I hope you are well. I have been working on a research paper that I think touches your
field. When a company collapses, does the damage spread to its competitors? The usual
answer is "it depends on how big the company was". My data say size is not the answer:
market share predicts nothing about how rivals do after the first day. What seems to
matter is whether the rivals **depend on the same thing** as the failed firm (the same
lenders, the same depositors, the same owner). That sounds like the efficiency-versus-
resilience trade-off in ecological networks. So far, though, my labels come from reading
the news by hand, and they found too few such cases to prove the point.

**In sustainability research, is there a standard way to measure how much a network's
members share a dependency (something like the "redundancy" or "window of viability"
measures of Goerner, Lietaer and Ulanowicz) that I could compute from data instead of
labelling cases by hand?**

With love and thanks,
Mrigay
