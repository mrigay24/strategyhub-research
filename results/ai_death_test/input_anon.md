# Classification task

## The rule

## 5. Classification rule (b)

For each case we write **one paragraph describing the shock as it was known on day 0**:
- what happened and why;
- what the firm does;
- how it was funded, where that matters.

The paragraph uses **no information about how rivals' stocks moved and no later events**.
The label is decided from that paragraph alone, in two steps.

**Step 1: was it the firm's own crash?**
- **Firm-specific triggers:**
  - fraud or accounting problems;
  - a default or liquidity failure of this firm;
  - a management or governance event;
  - a regulatory action against this firm;
  - its own product, plant, contract or lawsuit;
  - a short-seller report about it or its group.
- **Industry-wide triggers:** a policy, court ruling, tax, price war, new entrant,
  commodity-price move or demand collapse that hits the whole industry at the same time.
  These are labelled **"industry shock"** and kept out of the primary test, because the
  question is about a firm's *own* crash.

**Step 2: is there a shared dependency?** The label is **"shared dependency" (SD)** if at
least one of these holds; otherwise it is **"output-replaceable" (OR)**.

- **(F) Funding.**
  - All three must hold:
    - the failed firm is a financial intermediary (bank, NBFC, housing-finance company,
      broker, insurer);
    - the trigger is a funding or solvency failure (a missed payment, a default, a
      rating cut below investment grade, a failure to roll over debt, creditors pulling
      money, or news that it may not be able to pay creditors without a rescue);
    - the rivals are financial intermediaries that fund themselves in the same market
      (commercial paper, bonds, money-market or debt mutual funds, interbank loans, or
      uninsured deposits).
  - **Non-financial failed firm:** (F) applies only if the day-0 news names a specific
    lender or financier that the failure puts at risk *and* the rivals rely on that same
    lender.
- **(D) Depositors.** All three must hold:
  - the failed firm takes deposits;
  - the trigger involves withdrawals, a withdrawal limit, a moratorium or a regulator's
    takeover;
  - the rivals take deposits too.
- **(L) Liability or regulation.**
  - The trigger is a legal liability or a regulatory finding whose legal basis applies to
    the rivals too: the same law, rule, licence condition or liability regime, so the
    rivals could face the same claim.
  - A penalty for this firm's own misconduct does **not** count.
- **(O) Owner.** Tested through the group sets (H3), not the rival sets, because the rival
  sets exclude the failed firm's group.

**Tie-break:** if the paragraph does not clearly establish (F), (D) or (L), the label is
**OR**.

**Financial or not (for H2).** "Financial" means the failed firm is a bank, NBFC,
housing-finance company, insurer, broker or asset manager. Real estate is not
financial.

## The cases

**K01.** Firm X (US), 2009. A supplemental insurer. A note from a large investment bank questioned its capital, given $7.9 billion of hybrid securities (mostly bank preferreds). Firm X replied that its capital ratio was 425-475%, and its rating was cut one notch to AA-. Rivals: 4 S&P 500 life and health insurers.

**K02.** Firm X (India), 2019. A telecom firm of a business group that had shut most mobile services in 2017-18. Its board said 18 months of asset sales had failed to repay lenders (debt above Rs 46,000 crore) and that it would go to the insolvency tribunal. Rivals: 3 other telecom operators.

**K03.** Firm X (India), 2018. A jewellery maker and retailer controlled by its promoter. A large state-owned bank disclosed a Rs 11,400 crore fraud through letters of undertaking issued for firms of a diamond trader and of Firm X's business group, and enforcement raids followed. Rivals: 3 other listed jewellers.

**K04.** Firm X (US), 2022. Software-engineering services with a large share of its engineers in Ukraine, Belarus and Russia. Russia's invasion of Ukraine put its delivery centres at risk. Rivals: 4 S&P 500 IT consulting firms.

**K05.** Firm X (US), 2002. A utility holding company that owned a merchant power producer. A rating agency cut the producer to junk, triggering about $1.1-1.3 billion of collateral demands, and another rating agency put Firm X under review because it might have to support the producer. Context: the 2002 merchant-energy credit squeeze after the failure of a large energy-trading company. Rivals: 11 S&P 500 multi-utilities.

**K06.** Firm X (India), 2019. An EPC, roads and Delhi power-distribution company of a business group, hit by the same sales of pledged promoter-group shares. Rivals: 10 listed construction and power firms.

**K07.** Firm X (India), 2010. Firm X tests banking software for banks and insurers. It listed in 2009 and its price had risen steeply. The exchanges then moved the stock into the trade-for-trade surveillance segment, and it hit lower price limits on that day and the next. Rivals: the 10 largest listed IT services firms.

**K08.** Firm X (India), 2013. Firm X sells IT-based education services to schools and governments. Reports said lenders were selling Firm X shares that an investor had pledged and could not top up. Panic selling followed (about -60% that day), and the securities regulator opened a probe into the selling in several small and mid caps. Rivals: 3 other listed education-services firms.

**K09.** Firm X (US), 2009. A custody bank funded mostly by large clients' uninsured deposits and wholesale money. Fourth-quarter profit fell 71%, and investors feared it would need capital after moving troubled conduit assets onto its balance sheet. That day was also a broad bank sell-off day. Rivals: 5 S&P 500 firms in its sub-industry, mostly asset managers.

**K10.** Firm X (US), 2009. An aircraft (business-jet and helicopter) and industrial group whose finance arm was being wound down. Fourth-quarter results showed write-downs at the finance arm, a $625 million capital injection into it, job cuts and loss guidance. Rivals: 7 S&P 500 aerospace and defence firms.

**K11.** Firm X (India), 2015. A heavily indebted maker of forgings and castings. The stock exchange said the day before that it would drop the stock from the derivatives segment, after a quarterly loss. Rivals: 3 other listed auto-component makers.

**K12.** Firm X (India), 2019. A power producer. After a telecom company of Firm X's business group announced it would go to the insolvency tribunal, lenders to the promoter group sold pledged shares of group companies. Firm X itself was not reported to have missed a payment. Rivals: 6 other listed power producers.

**K13.** Firm X (US), 2024. A server maker for AI data centres. Its auditor (a Big Four firm) resigned, saying it could no longer rely on management's representations, after a short-seller report and a delayed annual filing. Rivals: 7 S&P 500 technology-hardware firms.

**K14.** Firm X (US), 2009. A regional bank. Fourth-quarter loss of $2.2 billion from a goodwill write-down and higher loan-loss reserves. Rivals: 4 other S&P 500 regional banks.

**K15.** Firm X (India), 2019. A business group's NBFC holding company (lending, insurance, mutual fund, broking), funded by bank loans, bonds and commercial paper. On the previous evening its auditor (a Big Four firm) resigned, saying its queries had not been answered satisfactorily; the company called the observations baseless. The group was under debt stress. No missed payment, default or rating cut by Firm X was reported on that day. Rivals: the 10 largest listed NBFCs and housing-finance companies.

**K16.** Firm X (US), 2009. A regional bank. Its fourth-quarter net loss was $6.2 billion, mostly a $6.0 billion non-cash goodwill write-down, plus a $1.15 billion loan-loss provision. That day was also a broad bank sell-off day. Rivals: 4 other S&P 500 regional banks.

**K17.** Firm X (India), news of 2008. Firm X builds roads, irrigation and metro projects. It is controlled by a founding family, who also founded a listed IT services company. On one evening, the IT company's board approved buying Firm X and an unlisted group property company for about US$1.6 billion. Investors objected that cash was being moved to the founders' family firms, and the deal was withdrawn before the market opened the next day. Rivals: 8 other listed construction contractors.

**K18.** Firm X (India), 2023. A city-gas distributor (a joint venture between Firm X's business group and a foreign energy company). On the previous day, after the Indian close, a short seller published a report on Firm X's business group alleging stock manipulation and accounting fraud. Rivals: 3 other listed city-gas distributors.

**K19.** Firm X (US), 2002. A health insurer with a run-off reinsurance book. After a reinsurance charge earlier, it warned after the close on the previous day that third-quarter and full-year earnings would miss guidance. Rivals: 4 S&P 500 firms in its current sub-industry, Health Care Services.

**K20.** Firm X (India), 2019. A small private bank funded mainly by retail deposits. The central bank had put it under prompt corrective action because its capital was below the minimum and its bad loans were high. It depended on a pending rescue merger with a housing-finance company, and that day it fell amid reports of central bank curbs and doubts about that merger. Rivals: the 10 largest listed private banks.

**K21.** Firm X (India), 2020. A North-India property developer with 52% of promoter shares pledged. It postponed the board meeting for its annual results by a month, and the stock hit lower limits for two days. Rivals: 4 other listed developers.

**K22.** Firm X (India), 2020. A supermarket chain of a business group. A disclosure showed promoter shares pledged for debentures of a group entity, and lower price limits followed about a week later. The COVID crash and lockdown fall within the next month. Rivals: 3 other listed retailers.

**K23.** Firm X (US), 2008. The largest US bank by assets, funded by deposits (much of them uninsured and foreign), wholesale debt and repo. It said it would take the remaining $17.4 billion of assets of its structured investment vehicles onto its balance sheet, deepening doubts about its capital. Rivals: 6 other large S&P 500 banks.

**K24.** Firm X (India), 2019. A housing-finance company funded by bank loans, bonds, commercial paper and debt mutual funds. On that day, reports of a public-interest petition alleging that the promoters diverted funds through shell companies hit the stock. The company denied the claims; a High Court listed the petition for later in the month. No missed payment was reported. Rivals: 7 other housing-finance companies.

**K25.** Firm X (US), 2009. A Midwest regional bank funded mainly by insured deposits. It reported a fourth-quarter 2008 loss on rising credit losses. Rivals: 4 other S&P 500 regional banks.

**K26.** Firm X (US), 2024. A life and supplemental-health insurer. A short seller alleged misconduct in the agency force of one of its subsidiaries. Rivals: 4 S&P 500 life and health insurers.

**K27.** Firm X (India), 2014. A generic drug maker. Its results, released after the close on the previous day, showed profit down about 78% because US drug regulator import bans on several of its plants cut US sales, and fresh concerns from the US drug regulator about another plant followed. Rivals: the 10 largest listed pharmaceutical firms.

**K28.** Firm X (US), 2021. A media group. Banks sold large blocks of its shares for a client fund that had missed a margin call. Rivals: 3 S&P 500 movies-and-entertainment firms.

**K29.** Firm X (US), 2003. A disability and life insurer. A rating agency report questioned how its low-rated securities and claims affected its capital; there was no downgrade or missed payment that day. Rivals: 5 S&P 500 life and health insurers.

**K30.** Firm X (US), 2000. A copier maker. It gave its fourth profit warning in five quarters, blaming its own sales and billing problems. It then cut the dividend and said it could not borrow in the commercial-paper market, while denying bankruptcy rumours. Rivals: 3 S&P 500 firms in its sub-industry.

**K31.** Firm X (US), 2007. A bond insurer whose business rests on its AAA rating. On the previous day, a rating agency moved its outlook to negative, and Firm X disclosed $8.1 billion of exposure to CDO-squared securities. The rating was not cut that day. Rivals: 5 S&P 500 property-casualty insurers.

**K32.** Firm X (India), 2019. A housing-finance company funded by bank loans, debentures, commercial paper held largely by mutual funds, public deposits and securitisation. It had been in default on some obligations since earlier in 2019. On that day it fell 20% on news of its exposure to a real-estate group and a failing co-operative bank. Rivals: the 10 largest listed housing-finance companies and NBFCs.

**K33.** Firm X (US), 2008. A life and annuity insurer. Hit by investment losses, it applied for TARP capital and to become a savings-and-loan holding company, while saying its capital was adequate. Many other firms also applied for TARP. Rivals: 5 S&P 500 life and health insurers.

**K34.** Firm X (India), 2013. A pharmaceutical maker, named in the same selling of pledged shares; it fell about 56% in six sessions. Rivals: the 10 largest listed pharmaceutical firms.

**K35.** Firm X (US), 2008. A health insurer. Third-quarter profit fell 53% on losses in its run-off variable-annuity reinsurance business, and it cut its outlook. Rivals: 4 S&P 500 firms in its current sub-industry, Health Care Services.

**K36.** Firm X (India), 2013. A garment exporter, named in the same selling of pledged shares in several small and mid caps. Rivals: 5 listed textile and garment firms.

**K37.** Firm X (US), 2008. A property-casualty and life insurer. It reported a $2.6 billion third-quarter loss on investments and halved its 2008 guidance; analysts said it might need $2 billion of capital. Rivals: 4 S&P 500 property-casualty insurers.

**K38.** Firm X (India), 2007. A road and infrastructure builder. On the previous day, the securities regulator issued an interim order barring the company and its promoters from dealing in the shares and opened an investigation into the price rise and promoter buying. Rivals: 7 other listed construction firms.

**K39.** Firm X (US), 2009. It reported a fourth-quarter loss, mostly from a large investment bank it had acquired. The government announced a second capital injection of $20 billion and a guarantee on about $118 billion of assets. Rivals: 6 other large S&P 500 banks.

**K40.** Firm X (India), 2019. A maker of transformers, switchgear and motors in a business group. It disclosed that an internal review had found unauthorised transactions hiding about Rs 635 crore of liabilities. Rivals: 3 other electrical-equipment makers.
